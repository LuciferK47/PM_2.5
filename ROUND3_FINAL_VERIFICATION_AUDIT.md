## B. Source-code fixes checked

- **LSTM alignment: fixed.** `lstm_window(z,C,lag)` pairs each lagged anomaly with calendar data for that exact week; `_lstm_recursive` appends each new forecast and matching next-week calendar feature. A toy deterministic LSTM produced aligned forecast sequence 12,13,14,15. **Residual concern:** in `lstm()`, horizon SD is calculated from recursive predictions at origins *within the same data that trained the model*. That yields in-sample optimism; a cap of one standardized anomaly SD is also heuristic. Do not claim calibrated LSTM intervals.
- **Daily simulation: fixed.** `week_offsets_by_month` pools complete Mon–Sun offset vectors with mean `exp(offset)`=1 (observed max numerical deviation 6.7e-16). `daily_paths` produces exactly the simulated weekly mean on complete simulated weeks. Calendar-year 53-step simulation covers Jan 1–Dec 31: 365 days (2019), 366 days (2020); monthly means and category counts use the same daily draws.
- **Bootstrap: fixed as implemented.** Blocks wrap *within* folds, with equal inclusion of boundary weeks. Inference still conditional on the tiny number of independent forecast origins, and should not be interpreted as a general ranking guarantee.
- **Linear regression predictive SD: fixed.** The code includes `Var(parameter estimate)` plus forecast error `m.scale`. Independent refits reproduced all 156 stored linear-model forecast means and SDs to floating point precision; forecast SD changes with horizon.
- **AICc convergence: logic fixed.** `_best_by_aicc` rejects fits that raise exceptions, report `converged=False`, or return nonfinite AICc. I did not repeat every computationally costly candidate optimisation or reconstruct the 6/3/5 rejection counts from the original environment.
- **Verifier's two previous date/index bugs: fixed.** Persistence includes the first week of each test year, and the 2020 January–June check uses each month's own observations and interval.

## D. Overclaims and interpretive risks

1. **'Most of the decline is real' is too strong** (`08_report_and_deck_text(1).md`, station-level subsection and Q&A). Six fixed stations, changing reporting networks, threshold >=60 days/year and only 125 days in the 2017 panel do not identify the fraction of decline attributable to actual air-quality change. Prefer 'the decline persists in a restricted fixed-station sample and after weather adjustment, consistent with a genuine component but not ruling out a network-composition effect'.
2. **'No model is significantly better than climatology' should be tied to the specific bootstrap design**, not stated as a general theorem. Only 2–3 overlapping calendar years of 52-step errors are available; a stratified moving-block CI is conditional on those years and resamples 8-week blocks, not independent long-horizon forecast origins. Prefer 'none showed convincing gains in this limited rolling-origin evaluation'.
3. **SARIMA near-boundary MA does not prove seasonal differencing is unnecessary.** Unidentified/unstable seasonal parameter and poor D=1 early-fold performance motivate D=0 comparison, but do not establish the true data-generating seasonal integration order.
4. **The LSTM prediction intervals should be labeled heuristic**, since its model is evaluated on training windows to construct horizon-error SD and then capped. Its point MAE comparison, after the feature alignment repair, is valid.
5. **Lockdown gap is descriptive model-relative, not an identified treatment effect.** The March 29 Sunday-ending week includes 23–24 March, before the reported 25 March lockdown; pre-lockdown overprediction is −11.3%, alternative levels −37 to −51% gap, weather-adjusted uses observed future ERA5 (oracle), 2019 placebo -14.9% wide interval. These cautions appear in the revised report and should remain prominent; any '95% interval' is model predictive uncertainty rather than causal-effect confidence bounds.
6. **Weather Granger significance is in-sample conditional predictive content, not physical causality or operational forecast gain.** The latest report states this reasonably well, though unqualified language like 'weather is therefore a short-memory predictor' or 'no usable weather value beyond a week' is stronger than a non-significant weekly test can prove.
7. **Poor-or-worse PM2.5 band is not the official multivariate AQI**; the authors added important correct disclaimers. In the first sentence of the report introduction, replace 'Delhi's air is Poor or worse' with 'Delhi's PM2.5 sub-index falls in the Poor-or-worse band' to avoid the initial misleading wording.
8. **Twelve of twelve monthly means inside intervals is not proof of 95% interval calibration.** Monthly forecasts are strongly dependent, their intervals are wide, and daily Poor-or-worse risk is overpredicted (55.1% vs 45.2%).
