## 4. Statistical qualifications that still matter

- A circular moving-block bootstrap of 52 dependent weekly errors within two or three annual forecast origins **does not create independent forecast years**. “Confidence interval includes zero” is properly interpreted as *no demonstrated advantage in this small experiment*, not evidence of equality or equivalence.
- The 2019 coverage improvement is a **single-year calibration check**. Five of 52 raw intervals miss below, none above; 50/52 covered after scaling, but mean score deteriorates. Conditional coverage at individual horizons or seasons is not established.
- The 2019 daily-Poor+ forecast is about **9.8 percentage points high** (55.0% predicted vs 45.2% observed). This may reflect both forecast-level bias and the within-week disaggregation assumption; the notebook's attribution solely to level bias is stronger than the evidence.
- LSTM interval spreads are obtained from recursive errors on training observations using the fitted network, with a cap; these intervals are heuristic, potentially optimistic, and not a comparable calibrated probabilistic benchmark. This limitation is now stated in the report.
- The *one-week* HLN p-values use lag h=1. Although the arithmetic matches, loss-differential serial dependence remains possible. For the same saved errors, a HAC intercept test (4 lags) gives approximately p=0.054 for lagged weather, 0.558 for prior-3-days, 0.105 for oracle; these sensitivity results illustrate that the p~0.07 values are not definitive. They do not overturn the finding of no convincing improvement.
- SARIMA with D=1 has Ljung–Box p=.016 at seasonal lag 52; the Fourier model also fails at lags 26 and 52 (p=.0010 and .0092). No claim of fully whitened residuals is justified.
- The ten-week “lockdown” average includes March 23–24 pre-lockdown days. All counterfactual gaps are descriptive: the model already overpredicts before lockdown, the 2019 placebo gap is −15%, and the level specifications move the comparison substantially. The report now acknowledges these issues.
- The displayed fixed-panel station statistics support a **directional sensitivity** but not a causal decomposition of the observed decline into true emissions change versus station composition. A reported correlation of .995 is high but not mathematical identity of city-day and station-mean series.
- The original `city_day.csv`, weather JSON and daily station panel are not supplied. Source re-building and exact station-pair calculations cannot be independently certified from this pack.
